# Coverage explanations (approved wording)

Use these on the `explained` page. Keep each to one or two sentences, adapt limits to the actual quote, and only include coverages that appear in the quotes. The proposal-ready short versions used in the approved proposals are in `examples/*.json`.

### Landlord / Homeowners

| Coverage | Plain-language explanation |
|---|---|
| Coverage A · Dwelling | If the home is damaged or destroyed, this pays to rebuild the physical structure — walls, roof, floors, built-ins. The limit reflects reconstruction cost, not market value. These are two very different numbers. |
| Extended Dwelling (+50%) | Construction costs spike after major storms when labor and materials are scarce. If a rebuild runs over the Coverage A limit, this extension covers up to 50% more so you're not left short mid-rebuild. |
| Coverage B · Other Structures | Covers structures on the property that aren't attached to the main home — fences, retaining walls, driveways, sheds. Set automatically at 10% of Coverage A. |
| Coverage C · Personal Property | Covers landlord-owned items at the rental — appliances, tools, furnishings you supply. This is not the tenant's belongings. Tenants need their own renters policy for their stuff. |
| Coverage D · Loss of Use / Fair Rental Value | If a covered loss makes the home uninhabitable, your tenant has to leave. This replaces the rental income you'd lose during the repair period. For a landlord, this is one of the most valuable coverages on the policy. |
| Coverage D · Loss of Use (owner-occupied) | If your home is uninhabitable after a covered loss, this pays for temporary living costs — hotel, meals above your normal budget, short-term rental — while repairs are made. |
| Coverage E · Premises Liability | If someone is injured on the property and holds you responsible, this pays your legal defense and any judgment. Without it, a single incident could expose personal assets. |
| Coverage F · Medical Payments | Pays a visitor's immediate medical bills after an injury on the property — no lawsuit required, no fault determination. It resolves small injuries quickly before they become claims. |
| Sewer & Water Back-Up | Standard policies exclude water damage from backed-up drains or a failed sump pump. This fills that gap. One event can ruin floors, drywall, and appliances — it's one of the most commonly filed claims we see. |
| Ordinance or Law | Building codes change. If a rebuild requires modern upgrades — updated electrical, sprinkler systems — your base policy won't cover those extra costs. This endorsement does, up to the listed percentage of Coverage A. |
| Loss Assessment | If the property is part of a community (HOA, condo association) and a covered loss triggers a shared assessment to all owners, this contributes toward your portion. |
| Water Backup (HomeShield) | Same as Sewer & Water Back-Up above. One of the most common claims we process — don't go without it. |
| Personal Property Replacement Cost (HomeShield) | Standard policies pay the depreciated value of lost items. This upgrade pays what it costs to buy the same item new today — no depreciation deduction. |
| Increased Replacement Cost on Dwelling | If rebuild costs exceed your Coverage A limit, this extension provides additional coverage to close the gap. Important protection in a rising-cost construction market. |

### Auto

| Coverage | Plain-language explanation |
|---|---|
| Bodily Injury Liability | If you cause an accident that injures someone, this pays their medical bills and lost wages — up to the per-person and per-accident limits. It also covers your legal defense if you're sued. |
| Property Damage Liability | Pays for damage you cause to someone else's vehicle or property — their car, a fence, a storefront. This covers the other person's bill, not yours. |
| Uninsured Motorist Bodily Injury | If someone hits you and has no insurance — or not enough — this covers your medical bills and lost wages. Most states have a significant number of uninsured drivers. This coverage matters. |
| Uninsured Motorist Property Damage | Covers your vehicle's damage when the at-fault driver is uninsured. Without it, you'd be paying out of pocket for repairs that were someone else's fault. |
| Medical Payments | Covers medical bills for you and your passengers after an accident — regardless of fault. Pays quickly, without waiting for fault to be determined, so care isn't delayed. |
| Comprehensive | Covers non-collision damage — theft, vandalism, hail, hitting a deer, a falling tree branch. Pays actual cash value minus your deductible. |
| Collision | Pays to repair or replace your vehicle after a collision, regardless of fault. Whether you hit another car, a guardrail, or end up in a ditch — this is what covers your repair bill. |
| Roadside Assistance | If your vehicle breaks down, runs out of fuel, gets a flat, or needs a jump-start, this dispatches help to your location. Small premium, big peace of mind. |
| Rental Reimbursement | While your vehicle is being repaired after a covered loss, this pays for a rental car — up to the daily and total limits shown. |
| Gap Coverage | If your vehicle is totaled and you owe more on your loan than the car is worth, Gap covers the difference. Important on newer vehicles with financing. |

### Disability income

| Coverage | Plain-language explanation |
|---|---|
| Monthly benefit | The check you receive each month (generally income-tax free) while you're disabled, usually about two-thirds of your income. |
| Waiting period | How long you must be disabled before benefits start. A longer wait lowers the premium; savings or sick pay cover the gap. |
| Benefit period | How long benefits can last for one disability. To age 65 or 67 protects you through your working years. |
| Own occupation | You're considered disabled if you can't do the main duties of your own occupation, even if you could work in a different one. |
| Partial (residual) disability | If you can work but earn less because of an illness or injury, the policy pays a share of the benefit based on the income you lost. |
| Noncancelable | The carrier can't cancel the policy, change it or raise your premium before the termination age as long as you pay on time. |
| Presumptive disability | Losing your sight, speech, hearing or the use of two limbs pays the full benefit even if you keep working. |
| Waiver of premium | While you're receiving benefits, you don't pay premiums. |
| Future increases | As your income grows, you can add coverage without new health questions, so your protection keeps up with your pay. |
